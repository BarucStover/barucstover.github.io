RESEARCH / MOLECULAR GENETICS

Functional Characterization of Siderophore Biosynthesis in Ilyonectria mors-panacis

This study investigates the genetic basis of siderophore biosynthesis and its relationship to fungal iron acquisition. The research integrates comparative genomics, biosynthetic gene-cluster characterization, and targeted genome editing to examine candidate genes involved in siderophore-associated pathways.

Experimental Workflow

01 Genomic Characterization

Identification and comparative analysis of candidate siderophore biosynthetic genes using sequence homology, functional annotation, and biosynthetic gene-cluster analysis.

02 Candidate Gene Evaluation

Assessment of gene identity, predicted protein function, domain architecture, and genomic context to prioritize candidates for experimental investigation.

03 CRISPR/Cas9 Experimental Design

Development of a targeted gene-disruption strategy using CRISPR/Cas9 ribonucleoprotein complexes to investigate candidate gene function.

04 Fungal Transformation

Establishment and application of fungal transformation methods to introduce targeted genetic modifications.

05 Molecular Verification

Molecular characterization of putative transformants to evaluate whether intended genomic modifications occurred.

06 Functional Characterization

Investigation of siderophore-associated phenotypes and their relationship to fungal iron acquisition and biological function.

Workflow stages describe the study's methodological framework. Completion and validation of individual stages will be documented as experimental evidence becomes available.
