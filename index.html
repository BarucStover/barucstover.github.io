MOLECULAR BIOLOGY · EVOLUTIONARY GENOMICS · COMPUTATIONAL INFERENCE

Molecular genetics, microbial evolution, and computational biology.

I study how microorganisms acquire resources, adapt to their environments, and evolve at the molecular level. My current work focuses on fungal siderophore biosynthesis and the evolutionary history of microbial iron acquisition. I also use computational approaches to investigate biological variation and collective behavior.

ABOUT / ACADEMIC BACKGROUND

About

I'm an MSc student in Biochemistry at the University of Northern British Columbia, with a background in molecular biology and chemistry. My research focuses on microbial iron acquisition, particularly the genes and pathways involved in siderophore production. Alongside experimental genetics, I work with comparative genomics and am developing an interest in mathematical models of biological systems.

02 / CURRENT RESEARCH

Research projects

01 / MOLECULAR GENETICS

Fungal Siderophore Biosynthesis

Investigating siderophore-associated genes and biosynthetic pathways in Ilyonectria mors-panacis, including computational annotation and CRISPR-based experimental approaches.

02 / EVOLUTIONARY GENOMICS

Ancient Microbial Iron Acquisition

Exploring siderophore-associated gene families in permafrost microbial genomes through comparative genomics and evolutionary inference.

03 / EXPERIMENTAL EVOLUTION

Bacteriophage Evolution

Research interests in bacteriophage T7 adaptation, genomic variation, and the molecular signatures of experimental evolution.

03 / COMPUTATIONAL MODELS

Coupled Oscillators and Collective Dynamics

An interactive Kuramoto-model simulation examining how coupling strength, natural-frequency variation, and phase lag influence collective synchronization.
